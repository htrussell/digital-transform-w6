Why this sequence

Capability first. Proof second. Scale last.

The phase logic is intentionally dependency-driven rather than a simple timeline.

Crawl · Foundation

The Crawl phase establishes three complementary execution layers: automated regional distribution for store replenishment, automated eCommerce fulfillment for shipped orders, and automated store-based fulfillment for pickup and local delivery. Walmart’s public disclosures show each layer increasing capacity, speed, accuracy, or digital fulfillment capability through robotics, machine learning, automation, and proprietary fulfillment technology. Placing these initiatives first gives later initiatives somewhere to act: AI can reposition inventory into a capable network, parcel stations can use stores as delivery nodes, and autonomous equipment can operate inside high-tech distribution centers.

Walk · Deployment

The Walk phase applies the foundation to limited production use cases that can produce evidence before broader rollout. AI-powered inventory positioning tests whether demand signals can improve where inventory is placed. Parcel stations test whether fulfillment-center volume can flow through stores into the last mile at greater density. Autonomous forklifts provide a clear pilot-to-deployment pattern: Walmart moved from a 16-month proof of concept to 19 units across four high-tech distribution centers. Together, these initiatives test decision automation, local-delivery integration, and human-machine operations before enterprise-wide scale.

Run · Scale

The Run phase scales proven capabilities across larger networks. Walmart’s grocery transformation extends intelligent software and automation into new builds, expansions, and a retrofit, while the geospatial delivery platform broadens same-day delivery coverage using more precise data-driven boundaries. These initiatives also require organizational change: associates move into technology-enabled operating roles, facilities adopt standardized automated workflows, and local fulfillment must coordinate across store capacity and delivery routing. The published outcomes—higher grocery throughput and 12 million additional households gaining delivery access—make the scale logic measurable.
